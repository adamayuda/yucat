# Why Cats Hide Pain — And the Signs That Give Them Away

*A dog in pain tells you. A cat in pain goes still, sleeps in a new place, and waits. Here is how to read the difference.*

![A cat watching from the shadows](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1478098711619-5ab0b478d6e6?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)
*Stillness is the most under-read symptom in cats.*

---

## The instinct behind the silence

Cats are both predator and prey. In the wild, an animal that limps loudly, vocalises and draws attention is the one that gets picked off. Domestic cats kept that wiring. Concealing weakness is not stoicism and it is not your cat being brave — it is an old survival reflex that has outlived its usefulness in a living room.

The practical consequence is uncomfortable: **by the time a cat looks obviously unwell, it has usually been unwell for a while.** Owners routinely report that a cat diagnosed with advanced dental disease or arthritis "was completely fine last week." It almost never was.

> Cats do not have a low pain threshold. They have a high tolerance for *showing* it.

---

## What actually changes first

Pain in cats shows up as **subtraction**, not addition. You are looking for things that stopped, not things that started.

### Movement

- Jumping onto the counter in two hops instead of one
- Choosing the sofa over the windowsill it always used
- Hesitating at the top of the stairs before going down
- Sleeping on the floor when it always slept up high

Height loss is the single most useful arthritis signal in cats, and roughly **90% of cats over twelve have arthritic changes on X-ray**. Very few of them limp. They just stop going up.

### Grooming

A cat's coat is a health report you can read for free.

| What you see | What it often means |
|---|---|
| Dull, clumped or greasy coat | Grooming has stopped — pain, obesity or illness |
| Dandruff along the lower back | Can't twist to reach it any more |
| One patch groomed bald | Focal pain underneath, or stress |
| Matting behind the shoulders | Reduced spinal flexibility |

### The litter tray

- Going *next to* the tray rather than in it
- Straining, or many small visits
- Crying in the tray

Litter tray changes get treated as behavioural problems and punished. In an adult cat they are a medical symptom until a vet proves otherwise. **Straining with little output in a male cat is an emergency** — a urinary blockage can kill in under 48 hours.

### Appetite and the jaw

Dental pain is common and almost invisible. Watch for chewing on one side, dropping food, approaching the bowl and walking away, or a sudden preference for wet food over kibble. That last one is a cat telling you biting hurts.

## Reading the face

Researchers at the Université de Montréal validated the **Feline Grimace Scale**, which scores five facial features. It works, and you can use a simplified version at home.

1. **Ears** — rotating outward and flattening apart
2. **Eyes** — narrowing or squinting when not sleepy
3. **Muzzle** — tightening from rounded to elliptical
4. **Whiskers** — pushed forward and straightened rather than relaxed
5. **Head** — carried level with or below the shoulders

A comfortable cat has a soft, round face with whiskers hanging loose to the side. Tension pulls everything forward and flat.

![A relaxed, loafing cat](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1519052537078-e6302a4968d4?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)
*The loaf with paws tucked and a soft face is a comfortable cat. The same posture with a tight muzzle and forward whiskers is not.*

## The purring trap

Purring means "I need something to change," not "I am happy." Cats purr while giving birth, while injured, and in the last hours of life. A cat purring at the vet is frequently self-soothing under stress.

**Never use purring to rule pain out.** Use posture, movement and face instead.

---

## A weekly two-minute check

Do this on a normal evening, not when you are already worried:

- [ ] Run both hands from ears to tail base — any flinch, twitch or turn to look?
- [ ] Check the coat over the lower back and behind the shoulders for dandruff or matting
- [ ] Lift the lip and look at the gum line for redness
- [ ] Watch one jump up and one jump down
- [ ] Note where they slept — high or low?
- [ ] Weigh monthly on a kitchen scale; **a 5% loss is significant**

Write the weight down. Trends catch what a single number never will.

---

## When to call the vet today

Some things do not wait for the next working day.

- Straining in the tray with little or no urine, especially in males
- Open-mouth breathing, or more than 40 breaths per minute at rest
- Hiding in an unusual place and refusing to come out
- No food for 24 hours — cats can develop **hepatic lipidosis** from short fasts
- Sudden dragging or weakness of the back legs
- Crying when picked up or touched in one spot

---

## The short version

Cats do not exaggerate discomfort — they under-report it. Treat any *quiet* change as loudly as you would treat a limp: a lost jump, a dull coat, a skipped meal, a new sleeping spot. You are not being paranoid. You are compensating for an instinct your cat cannot switch off.
