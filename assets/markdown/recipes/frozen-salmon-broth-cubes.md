# Frozen Salmon Broth Cubes

*A hydration trick disguised as a treat — for hot weather, fussy drinkers and cats on dry food.*

![Heart-shaped cubes set in a silicone tray, with several turned out alongside](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1627808814694-f2a18f68ed60?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)
*A small silicone tray gives the right portion size. Turn the cubes out once solid and bag them.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Makes** | 12–14 cubes |
| **Active time** | 15 minutes |
| **Freezing** | 4 hours minimum |
| **Keeps** | 2 months frozen |

---

## The problem this solves

Cats descend from desert animals and have a famously weak thirst drive. Wild prey is about **70% water**, so a cat barely needs to drink. On dry food that water is simply absent, and chronic mild dehydration contributes to **urinary crystals, cystitis and kidney strain**.

> A cat on wet food takes in roughly three to four times more water per day than the same cat on kibble alone.

You cannot make a cat drink. You can make water more interesting.

---

## Ingredients

- **100 g salmon fillet**, skinless and boneless — or tinned salmon in spring water
- **400 ml water**, unsalted
- **1 tbsp plain pumpkin purée** *(optional, for body)*

> **No stock cubes, no bouillon, no seasoning.** Commercial stock is loaded with salt and almost always contains onion.

---

## Method

1. **Simmer the salmon** gently in the 400 ml of water for 10 minutes, until opaque throughout.
2. **Lift the fish out** and cool both fish and liquid completely.
3. **Check the fillet obsessively for bones**, running a finger along the grain. Salmon pin bones are fine and easy to miss.
4. **Strain the broth** through a fine sieve to catch stray bone or skin.
5. **Blend a third of the salmon** back into the broth for aroma and body. Keep the rest for another recipe.
6. **Whisk in the pumpkin** if using — it stops the cubes shattering into slivers.
7. **Pour into a tray and freeze** at least 4 hours, then transfer to a labelled freezer bag.

---

## Three ways to serve

- **In the water bowl.** One cube flavours and chills the water without a full treat's calories. This is the daily version.
- **On a plate.** A lick-and-chase treat on a hot day. Expect it to be batted across the kitchen first.
- **Melted as a topper.** Thaw one over dry food to add moisture and tempt an inappetent cat.

Start with **one cube a day** — the 10%-of-daily-calories rule applies here too.

---

## Storage

- **Freezer:** 2 months sealed · **Thawed broth:** 2 days in the fridge, then discard
- **Never refreeze** a thawed cube
- **Label the bag with the date.** Unlabelled homemade cat food becomes a mystery within a fortnight.

---

## Variations

- **Chicken** poaching liquid, same method — milder and often better tolerated
- **Tuna** in spring water for a stronger smell
- **Plain water cubes** for cats that already drink well and just like the game

---

## Cautions

- **Never use commercial stock.** Onion, garlic and salt are standard, and the first two are toxic.
- **Bones are the real risk.** Strain the liquid and check the flesh twice.
- **Thaw slightly** for cats with dental disease or hard ice-crunchers.
- **Fish two or three times a week at most** — cats fixate on it to the exclusion of balanced food.
- **Check with your vet** for cats with kidney disease or on a prescription diet.
