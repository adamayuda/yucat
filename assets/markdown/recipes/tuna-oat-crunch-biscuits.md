# Tuna & Oat Crunch Biscuits

*Four ingredients, one bowl, twenty-five minutes. The smell will bring every cat in the building.*

![Homemade heart-shaped cat biscuits cooling on baking paper next to a storage jar](https://live.staticflickr.com/4414/37369909885_8ebd544dce_b.jpg)
*Aim for small, firm biscuits that snap rather than bend. Photo by Monika Animallama, [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/).*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Makes** | About 40 small biscuits |
| **Prep / Bake** | 10 min / 15 min |
| **Difficulty** | Easy |
| **Keeps** | 5 days airtight, 6 weeks frozen |

---

## Ingredients

- **140 g canned tuna in spring water**, thoroughly drained
- **120 g oat flour** — or porridge oats blitzed fine
- **1 medium egg**
- **1 tbsp sunflower or olive oil**
- **1–2 tbsp water**, only if needed

> **Nothing else goes in.** No salt, garlic, onion powder, stock cube or cheese. Onion and garlic damage feline red blood cells. Use tuna in spring water, never brine or oil.

---

## Method

1. **Heat the oven** to 180 °C (160 °C fan) and line a tray with baking paper.
2. **Drain the tuna hard**, pressing it against a sieve. Wet tuna gives a paste that will not hold a shape.
3. **Mash it fine** with a fork, leaving no large flakes.
4. **Mix in the egg and oil** to a thick paste.
5. **Work in the oat flour** a third at a time, until the dough leaves the bowl clean. Add water only if it will not come together.
6. **Roll to 5 mm** between two sheets of baking paper. Thicker biscuits stay chewy inside and spoil faster.
7. **Cut into 1 cm squares.** Cat treats should be genuinely tiny.
8. **Bake 12–15 minutes**, until the edges are golden and the tops feel dry.
9. **Cool completely on a rack** — they crisp as they cool.

---

## Portioning

Treats should stay under **10% of daily calories**. Reduce the main meal slightly on days you use them; this is where home-baking quietly makes cats fat.

| Cat weight | Treat allowance | Biscuits per day |
|---|---|---|
| 3 kg | 16 kcal | 2–3 |
| 4 kg | 20 kcal | 3–4 |
| 5–6 kg | 24–27 kcal | 4–5 |

## Storage

![Baked cat biscuits cooling on parchment beside a filled glass jar](https://live.staticflickr.com/4331/36557351423_10abf1507c_b.jpg)
*Cool them fully before jarring. Trapped steam turns a crisp biscuit soft overnight. Photo by Monika Animallama, [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/).*

- **Airtight jar:** 5 days · **Fridge:** 2 weeks · **Freezer:** 6 weeks
- Freeze flat on a tray first, then bag
- No preservatives means no shortcuts — if they smell sour, bin them

---

## Variations

- **Salmon:** tinned salmon in water, bones removed
- **Chicken:** 150 g plain poached chicken, blitzed, plus an extra tbsp water
- **Harder crunch:** roll to 8 mm and bake 5 minutes longer

---

## Cautions

- **Fish is a treat, not a staple.** Two or three treat days a week is plenty.
- **Introduce one new food at a time** so you can spot a reaction.
- **Skip** for cats with a fish allergy; check with your vet first for kidney disease, pancreatitis or diabetes.
- **Break biscuits in half** for greedy eaters.

---

*Home-made treats are complementary food. They are not complete and balanced and should never replace a cat's main diet.*
